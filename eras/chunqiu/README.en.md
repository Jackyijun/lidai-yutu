# The Spring and Autumn Period

[![中文](https://img.shields.io/badge/lang-%E4%B8%AD%E6%96%87-red.svg)](README.md) [![English](https://img.shields.io/badge/lang-English-blue.svg)](README.en.md)

An interactive historical map of the Spring and Autumn period (770–476 BC): the territories of the states at 46 points in time (opening with 772 BC, the year before Western Zhou fell); 60 states (Zhou, Jin, Chu, Qi, Qin, Lu, Song, Wey, Zheng, Chen, Cai, Cao, Xu, Yan, Wu and Yue, and lesser states such as Guo, Yu, Xing, Ji, Ju, Zhu, Qunshu and Xu) and 7 Rong and Di tribal peoples (Red Di, White Di, Mountain Rong, Western Rong, Rong of the Yi and Luo, Dali Rong, Huai Yi); ancient and modern names for more than 200 capitals, cities, towns and passes; and battles, covenants and cultural events, the Fangcheng wall of Chu and the Long Wall of Qi, canals, mountains and rivers, and the neighbouring peoples. The interface is bilingual: switch with the "中 | EN" control at the top right of the map.

The map runs straight into the [Warring States map](https://jackyijun.github.io/zhanguo-map/): the two share the same ownership points and the same travel-cost grid, so the last node here (476 BC) and the first Warring States node (475 BC) have pixel-identical territories and colours.

## 3. Build locally

Build from the monorepo root (see the monorepo README for the Python version and dependencies):

```bash
python run.py chunqiu  # this volume's stage 1 → recompute territories with the unified engine (engine.json) → v1 data → docs/chunqiu/index.html
python run.py --web    # no recomputation: rebuild the v1 data and new pages from each volume's source.json and engine.json
```

This directory's `run.py` is stage 1: from the historical content in `content/` and the base map in `geodata/` it writes **`source.json`** (ownership at each node, texts, entries and base map, without territories or colours; checked in), which the monorepo converts into v1 data. Territories are computed by the unified territory engine (the monorepo's `core/engine/`) from the ownership at each node, and the result is kept in this directory as `engine.json` (checked in); colours are set in the monorepo's `global/palette.toml`; the page is generated by the monorepo's `core/web/`. This volume's own territory computation, colouring and page template were retired in the third batch of the architecture cleanup (November 2026).

| Command (in this directory) | What it does |
|---|---|
| `python run.py` | Download the base map if missing → base map and the ownership of place entries (`build/geo.json`) → assemble `source.json` |
| `python run.py --html` | Skip the base map and ownership and only reassemble (when you changed only text in `content/features.py` or the English in `content/en/`) |
| `python run.py --draft` | Assemble even when English translations are missing or out of date (missing ones temporarily show the Chinese); for previews only. Without it the build stops and lists what is missing |
| `python tools/i18n.py status` | Count missing and out-of-date English strings |

After changing `content/`, rebuild the volume from the monorepo root with `python run.py chunqiu` (stage 1, territories and page in one go). Running `run.py` in this directory alone does not update `engine.json` or the page; if ownership has changed but the territories have not been recomputed, `python run.py --web` in the monorepo notices that the engine's input fingerprint no longer matches and stops.

The same input always gives a byte-identical `source.json`, so changes can be tracked with git.

The base map data (`geodata/ne/`) ships with the repository, so it builds offline; if files are missing, `run.py` downloads the pinned version (Natural Earth v5.1.2) from GitHub.

The Warring-States-style old template the page uses is in the monorepo's `core/web/old/chunqiu.html` (formerly this volume's `src/template.html`, moved there in the third batch of the architecture cleanup, November 2026), and `core/web/patch.py` gives it the shared shell; moving the two volumes to the unified template is left for a later batch.

## 5. How it works

> From migration step 7 (19 November 2026) the territory on the new page in the monorepo (`docs/chunqiu/`) is computed by the unified territory engine (`core/engine/`; the result is `engine.json` in this folder, recomputed automatically by `python run.py chunqiu` at the monorepo root). Ownership, text and entries still come from this volume, and the seeds are still the `content/seeds.txt` shared with the Warring States volume. The outer edge is handled as in the old build (`outer = "box"` under `[engine]` in `era.toml`): the computed area is the grid described below (97°–128.5°E, 18°–44.5°N), all land inside it goes to the nearest seed except islands that cannot be reached over land and lie well offshore, such as Taiwan; these, and the areas held by seeds that belong to no state, are left blank. Unlike the old build, costs follow the unified engine's terrain and the sea cannot be crossed, so territory is no longer drawn out over the sea. The outline of the Zhou realm in Suzerain colouring is taken from Zhou in the engine's polity layer (the royal domain together with the states that acknowledge Zhou). The two volumes share their seeds, costs and computed area, so the territory of 476 BC in this volume and of 475 BC in the Warring States volume is byte-for-byte identical.

```
content/seeds.txt ──┐
content/links.txt ──┤
content/features.py ┼─► build.py ─► build/geo.json ─► assemble.py ─► source.json ─► monorepo core/: engine territories, v1, new page
geodata/ (base) ────┘                                    ▲
                                    content/features.py + content/en/
```

### 1. Why the territories are computed

There are no boundary data for the Spring and Autumn period; what the sources do settle is mostly "such a town belonged to such a state in such a year" or "such a state destroyed such a state". So only the **place → state** ownership and the years it changed are written by hand (`seeds.txt`), and a travel-cost map lets the computer infer the boundaries between states, keeping them on ridges and large rivers where possible.

> What follows describes how this volume's old build computed territories, chose colours and rendered the page; those parts were removed from this directory in the third batch of the architecture cleanup (November 2026), when stage 1 was reduced to writing `source.json`, and the description is kept to explain how the old page was made. In subsection 2, steps 1 and 5 (land, rivers) and the last step (ownership of places) are still how stage 1 works.

### 2. Steps in the old `build.py`

1. **Land**: clip East Asian land from Natural Earth, simplify and encode it.
2. **Cost grid**: one cell per 0.05°. Land costs 1, sea 4.5; every mountain range adds cost according to its "barrier weight", and the ancient Yellow River, the Ji, Yi and Beiluo rivers and the Yangtze, Han and Zhang also add cost; a layer of smooth noise with a fixed random seed is added on top. This grid is identical to the Warring States map's; `COST_SHA` in `config.py` used to check it on every build.
3. **Multi-source shortest paths**: a Dijkstra search runs from the ownership points active that year, and each cell goes to the point that is "nearest to walk to". Nodes with the same set of points share one search.
4. **Colouring each node**: look up which state each point belonged to that year, merge the cells into each state's area, smooth with a Gaussian, trace and simplify the outlines. Labels go at the point of each state's largest land area farthest from its edge.
5. **Rivers and lakes**: Natural Earth rivers are matched to ancient names by their English names; the old course of the Yellow River, the Ji, Beiluo, Yi, Luo, Ru, Ying, Si and other rivers are drawn by hand.
6. **Place ownership**: each city, pass and battle takes the ownership history of the ownership point it falls under, as listed in `content/links.txt`, so the tooltip can say "held by such a state in such a year". Previously this was found by checking whose cell the place fell in. Since the third batch of the architecture cleanup (20 November 2026) the old partition grid is no longer used: the point each place falls under is frozen in that file as that lookup last found it. A newly added place must be listed there (the build checks). On 21 November 2026 the 17 places that the unified engine's partition would put under a different ownership point were checked one by one against the sources, and 7 of them were relinked (Yu's capital, for example, used to fall under Xiayang and so was counted as Guo's). Zhongmou was also relinked, following the Warring States volume. The notes are at the top of the file.

### 3. `seeds.txt` syntax

One point per line:

```
name longitude latitude initial-owner [year+owner ...] [^year] [~year]
```

- `·` means not held by any of the Zhou states (land of the Rong, Di, Man and Yi, or blank); tribal peoples have their own keys (e.g. `赤狄`, `白狄`).
- Years BC are negative: `-655晋` means held by Jin from 655 BC.
- `~-350` means the point stops taking part in the partition from 350 BC. Every point added for the Spring and Autumn states, the Rong and Di and the early Warring States carries it. From 350 BC on, the partition uses the original Warring States points plus the points added in November 2026: four unowned points in the northwest, Xiabian from 280 BC, Yongshi from 473 BC, and Gaoque and two unowned guard points north of the Lang Mountains from 214 BC. The retired points still anchor the borders when they are traced, so the Warring States borders after 350 BC differ slightly (about 0.1% of the area) from what they would be without them.
- `^year` means the point takes part in the partition only from that year. Five points use it. `^-280` is Xiabian, added on 21 November 2026, a county of Qin's Longxi Commandery, counted as Qin's from the year the commandery was founded; when it was added, the territories before 280 BC were checked and are byte-for-byte unchanged. `^-473` is Yongshi (see the next item); it starts in 473 BC so that the Spring and Autumn volume and the 475 BC junction node are not touched. `^-214` is Gaoque, added on 22 November 2026: Meng Tian took Gaoque in 214 BC, so the Warring States volume's 212 BC node now draws it inside Qin, matching the opening node of the Qin–Han volume. Two unowned guard points north of the Lang Mountains (`狼山北1` and `狼山北2`) join in the same year, so that the area that falls to Gaoque stops at the crest of the range. When these three points were added, the Spring and Autumn territories and the Warring States territories before 212 BC were checked and are unchanged. A point that has not yet joined the partition, like a retired one, still anchors the borders when they are traced, so after adding a `^` point the junction nodes must be checked again: `global/junctions.toml` marks the Spring and Autumn → Warring States junction `exact = true`, and `core/v1/registry.py` stops with an error if the territories at the two junction nodes are not identical.
- The last section of the file holds nine points added on 21 November 2026 so that tooltips and the map agree (Bulang, Qisi, Taolin, Hequ, Ying, Xi, Nanwucheng, Qinqiu and Yongshi). The owners these towns and passes show in their tooltips fit the sources, but the map drew them inside a neighbouring state, because of how the borders are smoothed or because of nearby points. Each new point sits at the entry with the same owners as the tooltip, so the map now draws them right (the Taolin point is moved to the south bank of the Yellow River, and Hequ is added on the north bank so that the Guo–Jin border follows the river). For Taolin Pass and Qinqiu the tooltip owners were corrected as well; the territory around the added points changed by anything from a few dozen to about 4,700 square kilometres. Two more points were added on 22 November 2026: Nandun (Dun's capital after the state moved south; the map had drawn it inside Chen) and Pingqiu (a Wey town where the assembly at Pingqiu was held in 529 BC; the map had drawn it inside Zheng).

下阳 111.3 34.83 虢 -658晋 -453魏 ~-350

### 4. Continuity with the Warring States map

- `content/seeds.txt` is identical in both volumes; the unified engine computes both volumes' territories from the same ownership points, cost grid and computed area (in the old build the geometry and barrier weights of the mountains and hand-drawn boundary rivers also had to match, checked by `COST_SHA`).
- No line changes owner in 475 BC, so this map's 476 BC node and the Warring States map's 475 BC node use the same points, the same grid and the same ownership: their territory polygons and label positions are byte-for-byte identical.
- States on both maps (28 of them, including Qin, Chu, Qi, Jin, Zheng, Wu and Yue) have the same key and the same colour: colours are set by global polity ID in the monorepo's `global/palette.toml`.
- The continuity applies to States colouring. Suzerain colouring exists only on this map, not on the Warring States map.
- When you change `seeds.txt` or the mountains and boundary rivers on one map, make the same change on the other and rebuild both.

### 6. Display rules (`core/web/old/chunqiu.html`)

- Battles are shown only for the **current period**, i.e. after the previous node (exclusive) up to this node (inclusive); the caption bar lists "Battles this period", and clicking one locates it.
- Events, walls and canals appear only once they have happened; capitals show only the current capitals and former ones.
- Tribal peoples have pale, dash-edged territories and italic labels and are not counted in the header.
- The legend lists only the states alive in this period and those that fell in it (struck through), tribal peoples last.
- Zoom levels: the full view shows only state names, capitals and battles; passes, major cities, towns and modern names appear as you zoom in.
- Two colourings, switched in the header: **States** (each state in its own colour, the default) and **Suzerain**. In Suzerain colouring the Zhou royal domain takes the Zhou colour; states enfeoffed by Zhou that have not claimed kingship acknowledge Zhou and take a paler Zhou colour with hatching (nominally under Zhou, self-governing in practice); states that claim kingship or stand outside the Zhou feudal order are independent and keep their own colours; Chu, after claiming kingship, sent tribute to the Zhou king and received his gifts, so it also counts as acknowledging Zhou from 671 to 607 BC; tribal peoples are unchanged. A heavy line traces the outline of the Zhou realm formed by the royal domain and the states that acknowledge Zhou.
- Views are chosen from a drop-down (All, Central Plains, Jin & Qin, Qi & Lu, Jiang–Han, Wu & Yue).

## 6. Common changes

| To do this | Edit | Then run |
|---|---|---|
| Change who held a town in a given year | the matching line in `content/seeds.txt` (syntax above); **make the same change in the Warring States volume's `seeds.txt`** | `python run.py chunqiu zhanguo` at the monorepo root |
| Add a town, pass, battle or event | add a line to the matching list in `content/features.py`, and record in `content/links.txt` which ownership point it falls under (checked at build time) | `python run.py` here, `python run.py --web` at the monorepo root |
| Add a time node | add a line to `SNAPS` (year, title, summary) and a short label to `SNAP_SHORT` | `python run.py chunqiu` at the monorepo root |
| Change descriptive text | the notes and node summaries in `features.py` | `python run.py --html` here (it will report the matching English as out of date; see the next section), `python run.py --web` at the monorepo root |
| Change the English | `content/en/*.json` | `python tools/i18n.py lock` → `python run.py --html` here → `python run.py --web` at the monorepo root |
| Change colours | the monorepo's `global/palette.toml` (by global polity ID; states on both maps share a colour) | `python run.py --web` at the monorepo root |
| Change allegiances in Suzerain colouring | `ALLEGIANCE` (add the English if you change the grounds) | `python run.py chunqiu` at the monorepo root |
| Change interface text | `U` in the monorepo's `core/web/old/chunqiu.html` (`zh` and `en` sets) | `python run.py --web` at the monorepo root |

## 7. English version

The page has both a Chinese and an English interface, Chinese by default; switch with "中 | EN" at the top right of the map. The choice is remembered in the browser, and adding `#en` to the address opens the English directly. In English mode the map is labelled in English only; tooltips, the index, the chronology and the gazetteer add the Chinese name in small type after the English. Search matches both Chinese and English, case-insensitively.

- **The Chinese is the source text.** The English is stored separately in `content/en/` by file, key and field: entries use the key `category:ancient-name` (a second entry of the same category and name gets `#2`), nodes use the year, modern provinces `prov:name`, fall-of-state entries `fall:year`, period aliases `alias:key:name`.
- **Fingerprints.** `zh.lock.json` records the first 10 hex digits of the SHA-1 of the Chinese each English string was translated from. When the Chinese changes, the English counts as out of date; `assemble.py` is strict by default and stops on anything missing or out of date, and only `--draft` lets it through.
- **Tools.** `python tools/i18n.py status` shows counts of missing and out-of-date strings; `todo` exports the Chinese still to translate; `table` exports a Chinese–English table `build/i18n_table.csv`; `lock` records fingerprints.
- **Conventions** are in `content/en/GLOSSARY.md`; they match the Warring States map's, and names shared by the two maps are translated identically.

## 9. Sources and reliability

- **Territories**: inferred from the *Zuozhuan*, the *Shiji*, the *Guoyu*, the *Bamboo Annals* and other sources, and from the general outline of the Spring and Autumn maps in volume 1 of Tan Qixiang's *Historical Atlas of China*; compiled with AI assistance and not checked line by line against the original texts. The overall picture and the key years (e.g. Jin's conquest of Guo and Yu in 655 BC, Chengpu in 632 BC, Bi in 597 BC, Qi's conquest of Lai in 567 BC, Wu entering Ying in 506 BC, Song's conquest of Cao in 487 BC, Chu's conquest of Chen in 478 BC) are fairly reliable; the extent of the early states and of the margins (the Rong and Di, the Shu states, the lands south of the Yangtze, Ba and Shu) is rougher.
- **Boundaries**: entirely inferred, schematic lines. They only mean "this area belonged to this state".
- **Lesser states and tribal peoples**: the period had more than a hundred states; the map draws sixty. Lesser states not drawn separately (e.g. Wen, Yuan, Ying, Jiang, Su, Zhuanyu) are merged into a larger neighbour. The distribution of the Rong and Di follows scattered records in the *Zuozhuan* and is schematic.
- **Disputed points** are marked "one view" or "location disputed" on the map, e.g. the Chu capital Danyang, Old Jiang, the early Zhu capital and the moves of Qi (杞) eastward; early Chu draws on both the Dan–Xi and the Jing–Ju theories. The ends of Sui (Zeng) and Xu are not recorded; the map shows them at c. 360 and c. 390 BC.
- **English**: names and notes were translated by AI and independently reviewed; conventions are in `content/en/GLOSSARY.md`. Where the English and the Chinese disagree, the Chinese prevails.
- **Base map**: Natural Earth 1:10m (public domain); modern provincial boundaries are reused from the author's earlier map.

Corrections are welcome.
