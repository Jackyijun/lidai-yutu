# Sui to the High Tang, 581–755

[![中文](https://img.shields.io/badge/lang-%E4%B8%AD%E6%96%87-red.svg)](README.md) [![English](https://img.shields.io/badge/lang-English-blue.svg)](README.en.md)

581–755, from Emperor Wen of Sui taking the throne from Northern Zhou to Tianbao 14 under Emperor Xuanzong, on the eve of the An Lushan Rebellion: 42 time points. The late-Sui rebels and the Tang reunification are broken down month by month; the 16 time points from 670 follow the rise and fall of Tibet, the Second Turks, the Türgesh, the Caliphate, Balhae and Nanzhao, and the ten military commands of the Tianbao era. It includes campaign and journey routes (16, among them the Prince of Qin's campaigns, Xuanzang's pilgrimage, Wang Xuance's mission to India and Gao Xianzhi's expeditions), historical and modern place names side by side, a timeline and a gazetteer. The 755 time point is shared with the first time point of the *Tang–Song transition* map and has the same territories. Bilingual (Chinese and English), a single web page with no external dependencies.

## 1. Just look

After building from the monorepo root, open **`docs/suitang/index.html`**. The page is generated by the monorepo's `core/web/`, with all data, styles and scripts embedded: no network and no server needed. Since the third batch of the architecture cleanup (November 2026) this directory no longer produces a page of its own.

- Language: switch with "中 | EN" at the top right of the map (in the Timeline and Gazetteer views, at the top right of the content area). The choice is remembered in the browser; add `#en` to the end of the address to open the English version directly. In English, map labels are English only; tooltips, the index, the timeline and the gazetteer give the original Chinese characters after the English name. Search accepts Chinese or English, and English does not need diacritics.

## 3. Build locally

Build from the monorepo root (see the monorepo README for the Python version and dependencies):

```bash
python run.py suitang  # this volume's stage 1 → recompute territories with the unified engine (engine.json) → v1 data → docs/suitang/index.html
python run.py --web    # no recomputation: rebuild the v1 data and new pages from each volume's source.json and engine.json
```

This directory's `run.py` is stage 1: from the historical content in `content/` and the base map in `geodata/` it writes **`source.json`** (allegiances at each time point, texts, items and base map, without territories or colors; checked in), which the monorepo converts into v1 data. Territories are computed by the unified territory engine (the monorepo's `core/engine/`) from the allegiances at each time point, and the result is kept in this directory as `engine.json` (checked in); colors are set in the monorepo's `global/palette.toml`; the page is generated by the monorepo's `core/web/`. This volume's own territory computation, coloring and page template were retired in the third batch of the architecture cleanup (November 2026).

| Command (in this directory) | What it does |
|---|---|
| `python run.py` | Check the base data → base map (`build/geo_base.json`) → allegiances at each time point and items → assemble `source.json` |
| `python run.py --html` | Keep the base map if it exists and only reassemble (when you changed only text in `content/` or English translations) |
| `python run.py --draft` | Assemble even if English is missing or stale (Chinese shown where missing); the default is strict and stops |

After changing `content/`, rebuild the volume from the monorepo root with `python run.py suitang` (stage 1, territories and page in one go). Running `run.py` in this directory alone does not update `engine.json` or the page; if the allegiances have changed but the territories have not been recomputed, `python run.py --web` in the monorepo notices that the engine's input fingerprint no longer matches and stops.

The same input always gives a byte-identical `source.json`, so changes can be tracked with git.

## 5. How it works

> From migration step 7 (17 November 2026) the territory on the new page in the monorepo (`docs/suitang/`) is computed by the unified territory engine (`core/engine/`; the result is `engine.json` in this folder, recomputed automatically by `python run.py suitang` at the monorepo root). Ownership, text and entries still come from this volume.

**Stage 1**: `build.py` checks the base data → `basemap.py` builds the base map → `compile_data.py` works out the allegiances at each time point from `content/snaps.py` and assembles the items → `assemble.py` merges in the English and writes `source.json`.

**Allegiance points from 670 and the 755 junction** (step 8, 27 November 2026): the 755 time point is shared with the first time point of the *Tang–Song transition* map (section 18 of the style guide, "shared allegiance points"). Inside the Tang–Song map's computation extent (72°–136°E, 10°–55°N), the time points from 670 on use the same set of points as that map: the 111 points only it had join the partition from 670 (`IMPORT` in `seeds_late.py`; coordinates, modern places and types are read from the Tang–Song map's `content/seeds.txt`, so they are stored once), plus, from 698, the old Balhae capital at Mount Dongmou; the 69 points only this volume had stop taking part in the partition after 669 (`RETIRE`; they are still shown). Outside that extent (the Aral Sea, west of the Pamirs, India, north of Lake Baikal) this volume's own points are used.

- `pins = "active"` under `[engine]` in `era.toml`: each time point protects only the points that take part in its own partition, so the territories of the 26 time points up to 669 are byte-identical to what they were before the points were added.
- `align_retired = true` under `[engine]`: when the v1 data is generated (`core/v1/engine_geom.py`), retired points take the allegiance of the territory drawn around them at each time point, and units left with only such points and no territory on the map are dropped from that time point (the Fuguo & Qiang unit from 670; the Chanyu, Andong and Anbei protectorates from 745; the Mohe in 755). The original allegiances and polity table are kept in `own_src` and `pol_src`; the engine reads those, so its input and fingerprint are not affected by the alignment.
- The Tang–Song map is authoritative for the 755 time point (`global/junctions.toml`): inside its computation extent the territories are copied from it, and each point's allegiance follows its 755 time point (`TS755` in `snaps_tables.py`). If the Tang–Song map's 755 time point changes, change this too; at build time `core/v1` checks the shared time point of the two volumes.
- 33 places that were region points in this volume but prefectures or towns in the Tang–Song map (Mingzhou, Wenzhou, Quanzhou, Rongzhou, and Silla's Wansanju, Sangju, Myeongju and others) are shown as in that map from 670, or from the year the prefecture was set up (`SHOW`).

> What follows describes how this volume's old build computed territories, chose colors and rendered the page; those parts were removed from this directory in the third batch of the architecture cleanup (November 2026), when stage 1 was reduced to writing `source.json`, and the description is kept to explain how the old page was made.

**The old build's territory algorithm** (formerly `src/territory.py`, with its parameters then in `src/config.py`):

1. **Grid**: 46°–148° E, 3°–60° N, cells about 6.4 km (0.057°). Sea and lakes of 0.15 square degrees or more are impassable.
2. **Cost**: 1 on flat ground, plus ETOPO20 slope and plateau elevation; mountain ridgelines are burned in as Gaussian high-cost bands (peak +8 for main ranges, +3.5 for minor ones), with a little sideways wobble added to the hand-drawn ridgelines first; the Yangtze, Yellow River, Huai, Han, Oxus and Jaxartes add extra cost; finally everything is multiplied by ±38% multi-scale smooth noise.
3. **Partition**: one multi-source Dijkstra from all 465 allegiance points over 16 neighbors (including knight's moves); each cell goes to the point nearest in cost distance, giving each point its "cell of influence". Borders therefore follow ridges and large rivers.
4. **Outer edge**: the outer edge of the map follows the hand-drawn line in `content/frontier.py` (from the north shore of the Aral Sea, along the northern edge of the Kazakh Uplands, the Irtysh, the Altai, the middle Yenisei, round the north end of Lake Baikal, then the Shilka and the Amur to its mouth). Sea, large lakes and near-shore islets go to the nearest point and the page clips to land; islands farther offshore with no allegiance point (Taiwan, Jeju, Tsushima) are left blank.
5. **Tracing**: each class's indicator grid is Gaussian-smoothed (σ 1.2 cells) and borders are traced where two neighboring classes' smoothed values are equal, linearly interpolated along cell edges, with junctions of three or more classes placed at the mean of their crossings. The border network for the whole map is traced once and shared by both sides, then given three rounds of Laplacian smoothing to remove small kinks left by the grid interpolation.
6. **Polygons**: the border network is polygonized, faces are assigned by cell center, and stray fragments merge into a neighbor; a smooth displacement field (about 1–2 cells, at two scales) then gives borders on the plains small river-like bends; finally GEOS `coverage_simplify` simplifies the whole coverage. Neighbouring cells share the same edge, with no gaps or overlaps.
7. **Merging per time point**: `compile_data.py` merges cells into units (states, protectorates) and into states according to each time point's allegiances; labels go at the pole of inaccessibility of the largest piece; geometry unchanged between time points is stored once (`GEO.pool`).

**Old page**: the former `template.html` was the front end of the *Tang–Song transition* map, reworked to the shared conventions for these historical maps (历史形势图统一规范), with the language switch done as in the *Song, Liao, Xia, Jin and Yuan* map; the old `assemble.py` first merged and checked the English with `src/i18n.py`, then replaced the four placeholders `__GEO__`, `__DATA__`, `__UCSS_LIGHT__`, `__UCSS_DARK__` with data and wrote a single file.

## 6. Common changes

- **Change allegiances at a time point**: edit that time point's `own` in `content/snaps.py` (unit → list of prefectures; things like `#HB` are region shorthands), then run `python run.py suitang` at the monorepo root (the territories must be recomputed).
- **Add a prefecture or helper point**: add a line to `content/seeds.py` (`name|modern place|longitude|latitude|type|region|name history`; type z prefecture, c town, r partitioning only), assign it in `snaps.py`, then run `python run.py suitang` at the monorepo root (the territories must be recomputed). Add the English for a new prefecture name and modern place to `content/en/seeds.json`.
- **Change names after 670**: edit `NM_LATE` in `content/seeds_late.py` (`year:name` pairs separated by commas; the build reads them up to 755) and cite the sources in the line's comment; they must agree with the names at the Tang–Song map's three time points 755–757 (`N=` in `eras/tangsong/content/seeds.txt`).
- **Change notes, battles, events**: edit `content/items.py` or `units.py`, make the same change to the English in the matching JSON in `content/en/`, run `python tools/i18n.py lock`, then `python run.py --html` here and `python run.py --web` at the monorepo root.
- **Change the northern frontier**: edit the `NORTH` polyline (longitude, latitude) in `content/frontier.py`, then run `python run.py suitang` at the monorepo root (the unified engine takes its computation extent from it).
- **Change style or interaction**: the page template is in the monorepo's `core/web/` (shared by the volumes; text and switches that differ between volumes are under `[web]` in `era.toml`); afterwards run `python run.py --web` at the monorepo root.
- **Change colors**: change the polity's light and dark colors in the monorepo's `global/palette.toml` (by global ID, see `global/polities.jsonl`), then run `python run.py --web` at the monorepo root; a polity has the same color in every volume.

## 7. The English version

Chinese is the source text. The English is written in `content/en/` under the same keys:

| File | Content | Key |
|---|---|---|
| `units.json` | states, protectorates, peoples: full name `n`, short map name `s`, note `note` | unit key (e.g. `唐`) |
| `labels.json` | names a unit went by at a given time point (`lab` in `snaps.py`) | the Chinese name |
| `seeds.json` | prefectures and towns: modern place `mod`, names in each period `n` | point ID |
| `items.json` | entries: `old` `mod` `note` `yt` `sides` `cmd`, route places `p` | `category:historical name` (e.g. `battle:虎牢之战`); entries sharing a name add `@state` or `@modern place` |
| `snaps.json` | time points: era name `era`, title `t`, summary `d` | `year-month` (e.g. `618-05`) |
| `misc.json` | modern province names `prov` | the Chinese name |

`zh.lock.json` records a fingerprint (first 10 characters of sha1) of the Chinese each English entry was written from. If the Chinese changes and the English does not, the build reports it as stale. Naming rules are in `content/en/GLOSSARY.md` (pinyin, how commanderies, counties, garrisons and protectorates are translated, foreign place names, lunar months, how battle results are written, and so on).

**After changing the Chinese**:

1. `python tools/i18n.py status` shows how many entries are missing or stale;
2. `python tools/i18n.py todo` lists the Chinese for those entries; add or revise the English in the matching JSON;
3. `python tools/i18n.py lock` records the new Chinese fingerprints;
4. `python run.py --html` reassembles `source.json` (strict: missing English is an error); then run `python run.py --web` at the monorepo root.

`python tools/i18n.py table` exports every Chinese–English pair to `build/i18n-review.tsv` for review.

## 8. Tuning

The territory parameters no longer live in this volume: the unified engine's general parameters are in the monorepo's `core/engine/config.toml`, and this volume's outer edge, computation extent, simplification tolerance and so on are under `[engine]` in `era.toml`; colors are in the monorepo's `global/palette.toml`. This volume's former grid, cost, smoothing and color parameters were retired with the old build in the third batch of the architecture cleanup (November 2026).

All that is left in this volume's `src/config.py` is paths and the base-map extent; the outer edge of the map is still `content/frontier.py`.

## 9. Sources and reliability

Territories are inferred in outline from the *Book of Sui*, the two *Books of Tang*, the *Zizhi tongjian*, the *Tang huiyao* and volume 5 of Tan Qixiang's *Historical Atlas of China*, by prefecture and along mountains and rivers; they are only indicative. The 755 time point follows the *Tang–Song transition* map. Prefecture names after 670 were checked point by point (the geography treatises of the two *Books of Tang*, the *Yuanhe junxian tuzhi* and others), with the sources cited in the comments of `content/seeds_late.py`. Loose-rein protectorates and area commands were mostly nominal, and the steppe peoples had no precise borders; the northern frontier is hand-drawn after the usual maps of Tang at its height. Modern locations are the site or the city it lies in.

English names follow `content/en/GLOSSARY.md`: places in the Chinese-character world in pinyin, places beyond it under the usual English form of their local name at the time; dates keep the lunar month and are not converted to the Western calendar.

Base map: Natural Earth 5.1.2 (land, lakes, rivers, modern national borders); rivers, lakes, canals, modern provincial borders and some mountain and pass entries are carried over from the *Tang–Song transition* map.
